A colleague missed a deadline. We talked about the deadline. Two months later, the same colleague missed another deadline and we had nearly the same conversation. That's when CPR clicked: Content, Pattern, Relationship. Three levels, and most workplace conversations get stuck at the wrong one. The framework has a cost (you spend time diagnosing instead of talking), but you stop noticing it after the third use.

## The deadline that clicked

The first deadline conversation went fine. We talked about what got missed, why, what changed. Conclusion: a particular dependency had been later than expected, the colleague had underestimated, lesson learned. Content-level conversation. Quick, useful, done.

The second time, we had the same conversation. Same shape, same conclusion, same lesson. I walked away knowing we'd be having it a third time.

The framework names this: we kept treating a Pattern as if it were Content. Each missed deadline got the "what specifically went wrong this time" treatment, which is the right treatment for a one-off and the wrong treatment for the third one in a row.

## What CPR actually is

Three layers, bottom up:

**Content.** This specific instance. The deadline that was missed. The bug that shipped. The meeting that ran over. Conversations at this level are about facts and root cause for one event.

**Pattern.** The shape that's appearing across instances. Deadlines keep slipping. Bugs keep shipping. Meetings keep running over. Conversations at this level are about why the shape exists, not why the latest example happened.

**Relationship.** What the pattern is doing to how you work together. The deadline-slipping is starting to feel like the colleague doesn't take their commitments seriously. Or you're starting to compensate by quietly padding their estimates. Conversations at this level are about the trust between people, not the work itself.

The cheap mistake is having a Pattern problem and trying to fix it with a Content conversation. The expensive mistake is having a Relationship problem and trying to fix it with a Pattern conversation. Both happen all the time.

## When to escalate

The rule of three works well enough: first time, talk about content; second time, name the pattern (gently, with curiosity); third time, talk about the relationship.

"Gently, with curiosity" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. The first Pattern conversation often surprises the other person. They were treating each instance as Content too. Naming the pattern is genuinely new information, not an accusation.

Relationship-level conversations are harder. They're the ones I avoid. The framework says: if the pattern keeps reappearing after you've named it, the conversation needs to move to what the pattern is doing to the relationship. I've had three of these in my career. Each one improved the relationship, but I dreaded all three.

## The cost and the payoff

The cost: you stop being fully present in the conversation. Part of your brain is diagnosing while the other part is responding. The first few times you try this, you'll go silent mid-sentence trying to figure out what level you're at.

The payoff: you stop having the same conversation three times. You also stop bringing Relationship-level worries to Content-level meetings, which is the other failure mode. The hidden third payoff: you start noticing when other people are at a different level than you, which is its own skill.

Most workplace conversations get stuck at the wrong level. Once you can name which one, you can move them.
