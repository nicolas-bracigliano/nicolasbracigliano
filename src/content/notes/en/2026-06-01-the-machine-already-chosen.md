The Barista Pro has the grinder built in, and I've run out of room on it. I want to push the recipe further and it won't let me: not enough control over the brew, a grinder I can't really dial in, a steam wand so weak that steaming the milk takes forever. By the time I'm done, the coffee is already cold. Or close enough.

So the plan was simple. A dedicated espresso machine, because most all-in-ones are mediocre. The Lelit Bianca was the one I kept coming back to: dual boilers, proper control, no waiting between brewing and steaming. But it cost more than I was willing to pay. Then I looked at the Lelit MaraX. It is considerably cheaper, and its heat-exchange boiler might be exactly the compromise I can live with.

Then the Meraki Gen 2 turned up. Grind and brew by weight, a rotary pump. That spec sheet made me look at an all-in-one with different eyes. It doesn't disprove the rule; most all-in-ones really are mediocre. But the Meraki seems to be the exception. The grinder is usually an all-in-one's weak point, and Meraki co-engineered this one with Timemore, which gives me some hope. Now I no longer know whether to buy a brewer and a grinder, or abandon the principle and get an all-in-one, knowing that I can always get a dedicated grinder if needed.
